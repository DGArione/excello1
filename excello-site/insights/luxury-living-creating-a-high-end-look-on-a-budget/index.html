Design on a budget

Thoughtful home design within a budget.

Practical design choices that read as considered rather than expensive.

A good home is not a line of expensive brands. It is a plan that works, light that falls where it should, materials that age well and details that are correctly made. Most of this is cheaper than people assume; the rest requires decisions early.

Spend on things you touch every day

Door handles, taps, light switches, floor material in the main rooms, the kitchen bench, the shower mixer. These are not places to save thirty percent for a saving the family notices every day.

Save on things you do not notice

You do not need the premium tile behind a cupboard. You do not need chandeliers in utility rooms. Many expensive finishes exist to be photographed; a lived-in house uses only a few.

Pick a short material palette

Three or four materials carried through a house look better and cost less than ten.

Get the ceiling and the light right

A plain, well-made ceiling with the right light fittings beats an ornate ceiling with cheap ones. Daylight and considered artificial light make a modest house feel calm.

Finish properly

Mitres, reveals, grout lines, door edges — the parts most people never notice are the parts that separate a decent build from a careful one.
