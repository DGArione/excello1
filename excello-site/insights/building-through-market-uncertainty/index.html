Planning

Building through market uncertainty.

How to plan a project when costs, exchange rates and material availability are shifting. Protect the brief, phase the spend, and avoid committing to the wrong number early.

The last few years have taught every property owner that construction cost is a moving quantity. A budget written in January can be out of date by April. The right response is not to delay the project; it is to plan around the moving parts rather than against them.

Separate the brief from the price

Your brief is what the project is supposed to achieve: number of rooms, how the house works for the family, the kind of place a hotel is for. The price is what it will cost to produce that result at today's rates. These two things change at very different speeds. Protecting the brief protects the project. Protecting a 12-month-old number does not.

Price by scope, not by headline rate

A single per-square-foot figure can be useful for a first conversation, but it hides where the money actually goes. On any given project, the finishes, the services, the external works and the client's own choices move the number more than the structural shell does.

Phase the commitment

Where possible, hold decisions until they must be made. If kitchen appliances will be bought at the end, their price belongs to that moment, not to the budget signed off before design began.

Keep an honest contingency

A real contingency is a sum you expect to spend, not an aspirational safety net. On a residential project in Sri Lanka today, planning without one is planning for a stoppage.
