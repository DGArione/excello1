Small plots

Planning a home on a small plot.

Setbacks, light, parking and privacy on tight urban land in Sri Lanka. A small plot still delivers a liveable family home — if the plan starts from the site, not from a template.

Colombo and the suburbs have a lot of small residential plots: 8, 10, 12 perches at most. The common mistake is to shrink a bigger house to fit; the better move is to design the house to this plot.

Start with the regulations

Setbacks, floor-to-plot ratios, parking requirements and height limits vary by zone. Confirm them with the relevant local authority before anything else. A good brief that cannot be built is not a good brief.

Make daylight a design decision

A narrow urban plot forces you to decide where light comes in. Internal courtyards, light wells, high windows and good roof design often matter more than the window area that faces the street.

Protect privacy without closing the house in

Screens, planting and considered openings do more for privacy than solid walls. Looking out onto a courtyard is almost always better than looking at a neighbour's wall two metres away.

Plan parking honestly

Parking takes real area and should be designed in from the beginning, not solved at the end.

Build vertically where the plot allows

In most Colombo zones a two- or three-storey home is permissible and often the right answer. Stairs eat area; a sensible stair in the right place pays for itself.
