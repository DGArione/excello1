Design-and-build or separate appointments.

What the two routes actually mean, how risk and decisions shift between them, and when each is the right call for a Sri Lankan project.

Clients ask for both approaches, and both can produce a good building. The difference is who holds which conversation, and when the hard decisions are made.

Design-and-build

One studio holds design and construction together. Decisions are tested against both sides from day one. Timeline is usually shorter, change management is simpler, and the single point of accountability makes resolution of issues faster. Fit is good for clients who want one phone number to call.

Separate appointments

An architect produces the design; a contractor is appointed later to build it. The client holds the relationship between the two. Fit is good for projects where the design must be fully tendered before build cost is committed, or where a specific contractor is required.

Where cost is really decided

In both routes, the cost of a project is largely set by the end of design development, not at tender. The route that includes a cost conversation earlier tends to end on budget more often.

Our recommendation

For a single home or a small hospitality project, design-and-build is often simpler. For a larger development or a public project with procurement rules, separate appointments can be the right route. Either way, decide on the structure early, in writing.
